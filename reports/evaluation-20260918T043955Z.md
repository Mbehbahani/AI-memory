Scope: diagnose and fix the two defects the P14 evaluation measured
(`reports/evaluation-20260917T193016Z.md`), then re-measure with the same instrument. The gold set
(`tests/evaluation/gold.yaml`) was **not** touched — not one character — and neither was
`tests/evaluation/run_eval.py` or `scorer.py`. Every number below is MEASURED by running A12's
unmodified runner against the live Gateway on the same corpus, minutes apart.

All four runs are kept as raw artifacts next to this file:
`reports/a09-baseline-expand.md`, `a09-baseline-noexpand.md`, `a09-after-expand.md`,
`a09-after-noexpand.md`.

## What was actually wrong (diagnosis, MEASURED on the live corpus)

The two reported symptoms had one root cause, and it was not the embedding model.

**1. The relevance signal was 30–50× smaller than the knobs applied to it.**
An RRF score is `1 / (rrf_k + rank)`: over a 40-candidate list it spans `1/61 = 0.0164` down to
`1/100 = 0.0100`, i.e. a total range of 0.006, and within a top-5 about 0.003. The boosts in
`config/retrieval.yaml` are ±0.10 to ±0.15. Adding them to an RRF score therefore did not *nudge*
the ranking — it **replaced** it: hits sorted into boost groups, with relevance only breaking ties
inside a group. Measured example, `/v1/search` for Q13 before the change:

```
score    rrf      boosts                                 source
0.1141   0.01449  {recency: 0.0996}                      Learning Resources - Senior AI Engineer.md
...
-0.034   0.01639  {recency: 0.0996, low_trust: -0.15}    Apache Tika Documentation ... .md
```

The last row is the **rank-1 semantic candidate** (cosine 0.654) and the exact document the question
names. AC-6 asks that clippings "rank lower"; implemented as absolute points it made them
unreachable. The same mechanism explains A12's finding 1: `entity_linked` (+0.10) is granted to any
chunk sharing an entity with the 1-hop expansion — a property of hub entities, not of topical fit —
so entity-linked generic documents outranked exactly-matching specific ones regardless of similarity.
Measured on the same run: `recency` was ≈ 0.0996 for *every* hit (observed_at ≈ ingestion time for
the whole corpus), i.e. 87 % of the score magnitude carried zero ranking information.

**2. RRF is rank-based, so the score was scale-free by construction.**
The top hit of every query scored `1/61` whether its cosine was 0.74 or 0.32. Adding a constant
recency term produced the flat 0.216 seen for every question in A12's report — answerable or not.
The system was not "equally confident about everything" because of the corpus; it had **no channel
in which confidence could be expressed**.

**3. The semantic retriever was already finding the right documents.**
Measured for the failing questions before the change: the expected source was the **rank-1 semantic
candidate** for Q13 (0.654), Q20 (0.640), Q21 (0.469) and Q22 (0.592). Those were not recall
failures. They were ranking failures introduced after retrieval.

**4. Nothing in the pipeline knew about term rarity.** A MiniLM cosine cannot distinguish "mentions
Portainer once" from "uses the same forty generic career words"; `ts_rank_cd` is term-*frequency*
based, which rewards exactly the long generic documents A12 complained about; and
`plainto_tsquery` ANDs every content lexeme, so on a natural-language question the keyword retriever
returned **0 candidates for 14 of the 24 gold questions** (MEASURED) and, when it did return
something, it was a document containing all the common words.

## What changed (`packages/aimemory/retrieval/`)

1. **New calibrated base score** (`relevance.py`): `relevance = 0.5 * cosine + 0.5 * lexical`, where
   `lexical` is the share of the query's **IDF-weighted** lexemes that the candidate's own text
   contains (coverage, deliberately not frequency — mentioning `Portainer` once beats repeating
   `data` forty times). IDF comes from the corpus itself (`ln(1 + N/(1+df))`, `df` counted over
   `chunks.tsv` with the same `english` configuration the index uses). Candidates missing from the
   semantic list are imputed that list's *minimum* cosine (an upper bound), not 0.
2. **Boosts became proportional** (`boosts.py`): `score = relevance * (1 + Σ fractions)`. The
   configured `0.10` now reads "+10 % of this hit's relevance" instead of "+0.10 points".
   `config/retrieval.yaml` is **unchanged** — no values were tuned.
3. **The `boosts` map is now a complete additive decomposition**: it carries the base components
   (`semantic`, `lexical`) alongside the scaled adjustments, so `score == sum(boosts.values())` and
   a stored `retrieval_logs` row still explains a ranking without re-running the query. The unscaled
   percentages live in `RankedCandidate.boost_fractions`; `w_semantic`/`w_lexical` are written into
   `retrieval_logs.params`.
4. **RRF keeps its documented job** — fusing the two retrievers' rank lists into the
   `candidates.fused_top_k` pool — and is now the tie-break rather than the ranking. Rank agreement
   no longer outranks a materially better match.

Same Q13 query after the change (MEASURED, live `/v1/search`): all five hits are the Tika clipping,
top score 0.580, decomposed as `{semantic: 0.301, lexical: 0.310, recency: 0.061, low_trust: -0.092}`
— the AC-6 penalty is still applied and still visible, as a demotion rather than an exile.

## Per-question deltas, including the losses (MEASURED, expand=true)

Gains: **Q13, Q15, Q16, Q19, Q21** went from hit@5 `no` to `yes`. Expected-type improved on Q14
(0.00 → 1.00) and Q16 (0.00 → 1.00); expected-entity on Q13 (0.00 → 1.00).

Losses, stated because they are real:

- **Q02** ("Which projects use Databricks?") expected-entity presence **0.67 → 0.33**. The hits are
  now the documents that literally discuss Databricks rather than career documents that mention two
  of the three expected project names in passing. Better hits, worse score on this particular
  metric.
- **Q18** (no-expand run) expected-entity presence **0.75 → 0.50**, same mechanism.
- **Q09** expected-type match **1.00 → 0.00** in both runs (the returned top hits are chunks rather
  than a `finding`-shaped artifact). Q09 remains a hit@5 miss either way.

Net across the aggregate: entity presence still rose (72.0 % → 78.0 % with expansion), because the
gains on other questions outweigh these two. Nothing was tuned to make that happen; the weights are
the structural 0.5/0.5 (see the sensitivity section).

## Five questions still miss, and why (MEASURED, honest)

Q01, Q04, Q07, Q08, Q09 still fail hit@5. For all five the expected source is **not in the fused
candidate pool at all** — this is now a recall problem at the retrieval stage, not a ranking one:

- Q01/Q07/Q09 expect the vault's two hub files (`AIOS/me.md`, `AIOS/Maps/project-graph.md`). Both
  genuinely contain the answer — MEASURED: exactly 1 chunk of each contains "railway", 4 and 1
  contain "jobpilot" — but each is 1 chunk out of 4,757, and neither the embedding nor the IDF
  coverage of a short question distinguishes it from the many documents that discuss the same
  projects at length.
- Q04's expected `localfs://…/joblab-de/docs/0{4,6}-*-decision.md` sources **do now exist** in the
  corpus (A12 reported Q04 as stale when they did not), so Q04 is now a genuine miss rather than an
  impossible question. It is left untouched, as instructed.
- Q08 returns `JobLab DE Lakehouse.md` where the gold expects `JobLab Overview.md`; both describe the
  migration.

I did not chase these. Getting them would require either a cross-encoder reranker over the top-50 or
a better embedding model, both out of V0.1 scope (ADR-0009 fixes the embedding model). Fitting the
existing knobs to five questions would be fitting noise.

## Things I measured and did **not** ship (negative results)

- **An OR-based, IDF-ranked keyword retriever** replacing `plainto_tsquery`'s AND semantics. It
  fixed Q09 (the expected chunk went from semantic rank 21 to keyword rank 1) but cost Q15 and Q21:
  hit@5 **60.0 %** vs 66.7 % for the shipped version, and the deliberately-absent group scored
  *higher* (0.468 vs 0.452). Not shipped; `candidates.py` is unchanged.
- **A per-source diversity cap** (max 1 or 2 chunks per source in the final list). Measured
  identical hit@5 (10/15) — it changes which duplicate chunks fill the slots, not which sources
  appear. Not shipped: no measured benefit, and it can drop the better chunk.
- **A larger re-ranking pool** (25, 40, or the whole fused union instead of `fused_top_k = 15`).
  Measured **60.0 %** vs 66.7 %: the RRF cut is a useful prior. `fused_top_k` keeps its meaning.

## Weight sensitivity (MEASURED, and why the default was not chosen by it)

| `w_semantic` / `w_lexical` | hit@5 | answerable − absent gap |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 / 0.0 (semantic only, scale fixed) | 8/15 = 53.3 % | +0.087 |
| 0.7 / 0.3 | 10/15 = 66.7 % | +0.125 |
| **0.5 / 0.5 (shipped)** | **10/15 = 66.7 %** | **+0.163** |
| 0.3 / 0.7 | 10/15 = 66.7 % | +0.209 |
| 0.0 / 1.0 (lexical only, whole-union pool) | 9/15 = 60.0 % | +0.337 |

(Offline harness, expand off, so the numbers differ slightly from the end-to-end runs above.)
hit@5 is flat across 0.7/0.3 … 0.3/0.7 and the group gap rises monotonically with the lexical
weight. Choosing 0.3/0.7 because it maximises the gap on 24 questions would be tuning to the
measuring instrument; 0.5/0.5 is the structural choice (two independent channels, both already in
[0, 1], no calibration set) and the sensitivity is reported instead. A reader who wants the gap can
see exactly what it would cost.

## Cost (MEASURED)

- Lexical channel (query lexemes + document frequencies + one coverage statement over the pool):
  **mean 13.8 ms, median 7.2 ms, min 2.6, max 35.1** (n=18, 4,757 chunks).
- End-to-end `/v1/search` with `assemble_context=true`: **mean 57.9 ms, median 58 ms** (n=18).
- Whole gold-set run: 1.6 s before → 1.3 s after (24 questions; dominated by noise, not a claim of
  a speed-up).
- Document frequencies are counted with a `LIMIT` cap (`DF_CAP = 20_000`) so a stop-word-ish lexeme
  cannot become expensive on a corpus much larger than this one. That cap is a safety bound, not a
  measured tuning: at 4,757 chunks it never binds.

## One residual worth naming: Q20

Q20 now counts as a hit@5 — the job posting it asks about occupies ranks 2 and 3 — but **rank 1 is
still the generic CV-writing workflow template** A12 complained about (MEASURED, live: 0.866 vs
0.847). Its IDF coverage saturates at 1.0 because a long template does contain every content lexeme
of the question; coverage cannot distinguish "contains all these words somewhere across 3,000 words"
from "is about this". Proximity-aware lexical scoring (`ts_rank_cd`'s cover density, which is
term-frequency based and was rejected here for that reason) or a cross-encoder reranker would fix
it. Neither is V0.1 scope; naming it is better than pretending the class of failure is gone.

## OpenAPI

`schemas/api/openapi.json` was regenerated (`python apps/memory-api/openapi_export.py`) because three
integration tests assert it is current. **Zero** of the resulting diff is attributable to this work:
a path-by-path and schema-by-schema comparison of the checked-in document against a fresh export
showed no changed path and no changed schema — only *new* paths that other agents added since the
document was last exported (`/ops`, `/ops/parts/{section}`, `/ops/review`, `/ops/runs` from A16 and
`/v1/mcp/audit` from A10, plus their request/response schemas). The ranking change alters values, not
contracts: `ScoredHit` is unchanged.

## Honest limitations

- The separation between answerable and unanswerable questions is **real but overlapping**: the
  answerable mean is 0.712 and the absent mean 0.513, but the highest absent question (Q23, 0.585)
  scores above the lowest answerable ones (Q09 0.291, Q07 0.378, Q22 0.406). This is a usable
  ordering signal, **not** a reliable abstention threshold, and no threshold is proposed here — the
  corpus does not contain the labelled data that would justify one. n=3 for the absent group; three
  questions cannot calibrate anything.
- `k=5` and n=15 for hit@5. A single question is 6.7 percentage points. Treat the direction as
  measured and the second decimal as noise.
- The scores before and after are on different scales, as stated above.
- The remaining ceiling is the embedding model plus the absence of a reranker, which is the honest
  answer to "why not higher than 66.7 %".
